Uniform Asymptotics

Author

David Malison

Last modified

July 30, 2026

Let \(\Theta\) be a parameter space and, for each \(\theta\in\Theta\), let \(\mathbb P_\theta\) denote a probability measure. This note collects notation and auxiliary results for asymptotic statements that hold uniformly over the family \(\mathcal P=\{\mathbb P_\theta:\theta\in\Theta\}\).

We first define notation for uniform convergence in probability. We say \(X_n \in \mathbb{R}^k\) converges in \(\mathcal{P}\) to \(X \in \mathbb{R}^k\), written as \(X_n \stackrel{\mathcal{P}}{\to} X\), if for every \(\epsilon > 0\), \[ \lim_{n \to \infty} \sup_{\theta \in \Theta} \mathbb{P}_\theta(\|X_n - X\| > \epsilon) = 0. \] For a positive deterministic sequence \(r_n\), we write \(X_n=o_{\mathcal{P}}(r_n)\) if \(X_n/r_n \stackrel{\mathcal{P}}{\to} 0\). We write \(X_n=O_{\mathcal{P}}(r_n)\) if, for every \(\epsilon > 0\), there exist finite constants \(M\) and \(N\) such that \[ \sup_{\theta\in\Theta} \mathbb{P}_\theta\left(\|X_n\|>Mr_n\right) <\epsilon \] for all \(n\geq N\). The next proposition collects useful stochastic-order relationships:

Proposition 1 Let \(r_n\) and \(s_n\) be positive deterministic sequences. The following relationships hold whenever the indicated operations are well-defined:

(i) \(X_n=O_{\mathcal{P}}(r_n),\ Y_n=O_{\mathcal{P}}(s_n) \quad\Rightarrow\quad X_n+Y_n=O_{\mathcal{P}}(r_n+s_n)\).

(ii) \(X_n=O_{\mathcal{P}}(r_n),\ Y_n=O_{\mathcal{P}}(s_n) \quad\Rightarrow\quad X_nY_n=O_{\mathcal{P}}(r_ns_n)\).

(iii) \(X_n=o_{\mathcal{P}}(r_n),\ Y_n=O_{\mathcal{P}}(s_n) \quad\Rightarrow\quad X_nY_n=o_{\mathcal{P}}(r_ns_n)\).

(iv) \(X_n=O_{\mathcal{P}}(r_n),\ r_n\to 0 \quad\Rightarrow\quad X_n=o_{\mathcal{P}}(1)\).

Proof. For \(M>0\), write \(A_n^M=\{\|X_n\|>Mr_n\}\) and \(B_n^M=\{\|Y_n\|>Ms_n\}\).

(i) Fix \(\epsilon>0\). By the two stochastic-order assumptions, we can choose finite constants \(M_X\) and \(M_Y\) and an index \(N\) so that

\[ \sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\mathbb{P}_\theta\left(A_n^{M_X}\right)<\epsilon/2, \qquad \sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\mathbb{P}_\theta\left(B_n^{M_Y}\right)<\epsilon/2 \]

for all \(n\geq N\). Taking \(M=\max\{M_X,M_Y\}\), the same bounds hold with \(M\) in place of \(M_X\) and \(M_Y\). On \(\left(A_n^{M}\right)^c\cap\left(B_n^{M}\right)^c\) we have \(\|X_n+Y_n\|\leq\|X_n\|+\|Y_n\|\leq M(r_n+s_n)\), so

\[ \sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\mathbb{P}_\theta\bigl(\|X_n+Y_n\|>M(r_n+s_n)\bigr) \leq \sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\mathbb{P}_\theta\left(A_n^{M}\right) + \sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\mathbb{P}_\theta\left(B_n^{M}\right) <\epsilon \]

for \(n\geq N\). Hence \(X_n+Y_n=O_{\mathcal{P}}(r_n+s_n)\).

(ii) With the same \(M\) and \(N\), on \(\left(A_n^{M}\right)^c\cap\left(B_n^{M}\right)^c\) we have \(\|X_nY_n\|\leq\|X_n\|\,\|Y_n\|\leq M^2 r_ns_n\), so \(\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\mathbb{P}_\theta(\|X_nY_n\|>M^2 r_ns_n)<\epsilon\) for \(n\geq N\). Hence \(X_nY_n=O_{\mathcal{P}}(r_ns_n)\).

(iii) Suppose \(X_n=o_{\mathcal{P}}(r_n)\), and fix \(\epsilon>0\) and \(\delta>0\). Since \(Y_n=O_{\mathcal{P}}(s_n)\), we can choose \(M\) and \(N\) such that

\[ \sup_{\theta\in\Theta} \mathbb{P}_\theta\left(B_n^{M}\right)<\delta \]

for all \(n\geq N\). On \(\left(B_n^{M}\right)^c\), the event \(\|X_nY_n\|>\epsilon r_ns_n\) implies \(\|X_n\|>(\epsilon/M)r_n\), so

\[ \mathbb{P}_\theta(\|X_nY_n\|>\epsilon r_ns_n) \leq \mathbb{P}_\theta\bigl(\|X_n\|>(\epsilon/M)r_n\bigr) +\mathbb{P}_\theta\left(B_n^{M}\right). \]

Taking the supremum over \(\theta\) and the limit superior gives

\[ \begin{aligned} &\limsup_{n\to\infty} \sup_{\theta\in\Theta} \mathbb{P}_\theta(\|X_nY_n\|>\epsilon r_ns_n) \\ &\qquad\leq \limsup_{n\to\infty} \sup_{\theta\in\Theta} \mathbb{P}_\theta\bigl(\|X_n\|>(\epsilon/M)r_n\bigr) + \limsup_{n\to\infty} \sup_{\theta\in\Theta} \mathbb{P}_\theta\left(B_n^{M}\right) \leq \delta. \end{aligned} \]

Taking \(\delta\downarrow0\), we can conclude \(X_nY_n=o_{\mathcal{P}}(r_ns_n)\).

(iv) Suppose \(X_n=O_{\mathcal{P}}(r_n)\) with \(r_n\to 0\), and fix \(\delta>0\) and \(\epsilon>0\). Choose \(M\) and \(N_0\) with \(\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\mathbb{P}_\theta\left(A_n^{M}\right)<\epsilon\) for \(n\geq N_0\). Since \(r_n\to 0\), there is \(N_1\) with \(Mr_n<\delta\) for \(n\geq N_1\). Therefore \(\{\|X_n\|>\delta\}\subseteq A_n^{M}\) and \(\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\mathbb{P}_\theta(\|X_n\|>\delta)<\epsilon\) for \(n\geq\max(N_0,N_1)\). Since \(\epsilon>0\) was arbitrary, \(X_n=o_{\mathcal{P}}(1)\). \(\square\)

For each positive integer \(q\), define the class of functions \[ \mathcal G_q = \left\{ g:\mathbb R^q\to\mathbb R: |g(x)|\leq1, |g(x)-g(y)|\leq\|x-y\| \text{ for all }x,y\in\mathbb R^q \right\}. \] When the dimension is clear, we write \(\mathcal G\) for \(\mathcal G_q\). For \(X_n,Z_n\in\mathbb R^q\), we say \(X_n\) is asymptotically equivalent in \(\mathcal{P}\) to \(Z_n\), written \(X_n\stackrel{\mathcal{P}}{\approx}Z_n\), if \[ \lim_{n\to\infty} \sup_{\theta\in\Theta} \sup_{g\in\mathcal G_q} \left| \mathbb{E}_\theta[g(X_n)] - \mathbb{E}_\theta[g(Z_n)] \right| =0. \]

The next proposition shows that asymptotic equivalence preserves uniform stochastic boundedness:

Proposition 2 If \(X_n\stackrel{\mathcal P}{\approx}Z_n\), then \[ X_n=O_{\mathcal P}(1) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad Z_n=O_{\mathcal P}(1). \]

Proof. Suppose \(Z_n=O_{\mathcal P}(1)\). For \(M>1\), define \[ g_M(x) = \min\left\{1,(\|x\|-M+1)_+\right\}. \] The function \(g_M\) belongs to \(\mathcal G_q\) and satisfies \[ \mathbf 1\{\|x\|>M\} \leq g_M(x) \leq \mathbf 1\{\|x\|>M-1\}. \] Therefore, \[ \begin{aligned} \sup_{\theta\in\Theta} \mathbb P_\theta(\|X_n\|>M) &\leq \sup_{\theta\in\Theta} \mathbb E_\theta[g_M(X_n)] \\ &\leq \sup_{\theta\in\Theta} \mathbb E_\theta[g_M(Z_n)] + \sup_{\theta\in\Theta} \sup_{g\in\mathcal G_q} \left| \mathbb E_\theta[g(X_n)] - \mathbb E_\theta[g(Z_n)] \right| \\ &\leq \sup_{\theta\in\Theta} \mathbb P_\theta(\|Z_n\|>M-1) +\sup_{\theta\in\Theta} \sup_{g\in\mathcal G_q} \left| \mathbb E_\theta[g(X_n)] - \mathbb E_\theta[g(Z_n)] \right|. \end{aligned} \] Given \(\epsilon>0\), uniform stochastic boundedness of \(Z_n\) allows us to pick a finite \(M>1\) so that the first term on the final line is eventually smaller than \(\epsilon/2\). Asymptotic equivalence makes the second term eventually smaller than \(\epsilon/2\). Hence \(X_n=O_{\mathcal P}(1)\). The reverse implication follows by interchanging \(X_n\) and \(Z_n\). \(\square\)

The next proposition connects asymptotic equivalence and convergence to a fixed distribution:

Proposition 3 For any sequences \(n_k\to\infty\) and \(\theta_k\in\Theta\) and any fixed distribution function \(F\), the following statements hold:

(i) If \(X_n\stackrel{\mathcal{P}}{\approx}Z_n\) and \[\lim_{k\to\infty} \left| \mathbb{P}_{\theta_k}(Z_{n_k}\leq\mathbf t)-F(\mathbf t) \right| =0 \quad\text{for every continuity point $\mathbf t$ of $F$}, \] then \[ \lim_{k\to\infty} \left| \mathbb{P}_{\theta_k}(X_{n_k}\leq\mathbf t)-F(\mathbf t) \right| =0 \quad\text{for every continuity point $\mathbf t$ of $F$}. \]

(ii) If \[ \begin{aligned} \lim_{k \to \infty} \left| \mathbb{P}_{\theta_k}(X_{n_k}\leq\mathbf t)-F(\mathbf t) \right| &=0, \\ \lim_{k \to \infty} \left| \mathbb{P}_{\theta_k}(Z_{n_k}\leq\mathbf t)-F(\mathbf t) \right| &=0 \end{aligned} \] for every continuity point \(\mathbf t\) of \(F\), then \[ \lim_{k\to\infty} \sup_{g\in\mathcal G} \left| \mathbb{E}_{\theta_k}[g(X_{n_k})] - \mathbb{E}_{\theta_k}[g(Z_{n_k})] \right| =0. \]

(iii) Suppose \(Z_n\) has distribution function \(F\) under \(\mathbb P_\theta\) for every \(n\) and \(\theta\). Then \(X_n\stackrel{\mathcal{P}}{\approx}Z_n\) if and only if, for every continuity point \(\mathbf t\in\mathbb R^q\) of \(F\), \[ \lim_{n\to\infty} \sup_{\theta\in\Theta} \left| \mathbb{P}_\theta(X_n\leq\mathbf t)-F(\mathbf t) \right| =0. \]

Proof.

(i) The premise says that, under \(\mathbb{P}_{\theta_k}\), \(Z_{n_k}\) converges weakly to the fixed distribution with distribution function \(F\). By the bounded-Lipschitz characterization of weak convergence, this implies \[ \lim_{k\to\infty} \sup_{g\in\mathcal G} \left| \mathbb{E}_{\theta_k}[g(Z_{n_k})] - \int_{\mathbb R^q}g(\mathbf z)\,dF(\mathbf z) \right| =0. \] Moreover, for every \(k\), the triangle inequality gives \[ \begin{aligned} &\sup_{g\in\mathcal G} \left| \mathbb{E}_{\theta_k}[g(X_{n_k})] - \int_{\mathbb R^q}g(\mathbf z)\,dF(\mathbf z) \right| \\ &\qquad\leq \sup_{g\in\mathcal G} \left| \mathbb{E}_{\theta_k}[g(X_{n_k})] - \mathbb{E}_{\theta_k}[g(Z_{n_k})] \right| \\ &\qquad\quad+ \sup_{g\in\mathcal G} \left| \mathbb{E}_{\theta_k}[g(Z_{n_k})] - \int_{\mathbb R^q}g(\mathbf z)\,dF(\mathbf z) \right|. \end{aligned} \] The first term on the right tends to zero because \(X_n\stackrel{\mathcal{P}}{\approx}Z_n\), and the second tends to zero by the preceding display. Therefore \[ \lim_{k\to\infty} \sup_{g\in\mathcal G} \left| \mathbb{E}_{\theta_k}[g(X_{n_k})] - \int_{\mathbb R^q}g(\mathbf z)\,dF(\mathbf z) \right| =0. \] Thus \(X_{n_k}\) converges weakly to \(F\) under \(\mathbb{P}_{\theta_k}\). The claimed convergence of distribution functions at every continuity point of \(F\) follows from the portmanteau theorem.

(ii) The two premises imply that, under \(\mathbb{P}_{\theta_k}\), both \(X_{n_k}\) and \(Z_{n_k}\) converge weakly to the fixed distribution with distribution function \(F\). The bounded-Lipschitz characterization of weak convergence therefore gives \[ \begin{aligned} \lim_{k\to\infty} \sup_{g\in\mathcal G} \left| \mathbb{E}_{\theta_k}[g(X_{n_k})] - \int_{\mathbb R^q}g(\mathbf z)\,dF(\mathbf z) \right|&=0, \\ \lim_{k\to\infty} \sup_{g\in\mathcal G} \left| \mathbb{E}_{\theta_k}[g(Z_{n_k})] - \int_{\mathbb R^q}g(\mathbf z)\,dF(\mathbf z) \right|&=0. \end{aligned} \] By the triangle inequality, \[ \begin{aligned} &\sup_{g\in\mathcal G} \left| \mathbb{E}_{\theta_k}[g(X_{n_k})] - \mathbb{E}_{\theta_k}[g(Z_{n_k})] \right| \\ &\qquad\leq \sup_{g\in\mathcal G} \left| \mathbb{E}_{\theta_k}[g(X_{n_k})] - \int_{\mathbb R^q}g(\mathbf z)\,dF(\mathbf z) \right| \\ &\qquad\quad+ \sup_{g\in\mathcal G} \left| \mathbb{E}_{\theta_k}[g(Z_{n_k})] - \int_{\mathbb R^q}g(\mathbf z)\,dF(\mathbf z) \right|. \end{aligned} \] Both terms on the right tend to zero, so \[ \lim_{k\to\infty} \sup_{g\in\mathcal G} \left| \mathbb{E}_{\theta_k}[g(X_{n_k})] - \mathbb{E}_{\theta_k}[g(Z_{n_k})] \right| =0. \]

(iii) First suppose \(X_n\stackrel{\mathcal P}{\approx}Z_n\). If the asserted uniform convergence failed, there would be a continuity point \(\mathbf t\) of \(F\), an \(\epsilon>0\), and sequences \(n_k\to\infty\) and \(\theta_k\in\Theta\) such that \[ \left| \mathbb P_{\theta_k}(X_{n_k}\leq\mathbf t) -F(\mathbf t) \right| \geq\epsilon \] for every \(k\). Because \(Z_{n_k}\) has distribution function \(F\) under \(\mathbb P_{\theta_k}\), part (i) implies that the left-hand side converges to zero, a contradiction.

Conversely, suppose the asserted uniform convergence holds. If \(X_n\stackrel{\mathcal P}{\approx}Z_n\) failed, there would be an \(\epsilon>0\) and sequences \(n_k\to\infty\) and \(\theta_k\in\Theta\) such that \[ \sup_{g\in\mathcal G} \left| \mathbb E_{\theta_k}[g(X_{n_k})] - \mathbb E_{\theta_k}[g(Z_{n_k})] \right| \geq\epsilon \] for every \(k\). The asserted uniform convergence implies that \(X_{n_k}\) converges weakly to \(F\) under \(\mathbb P_{\theta_k}\), while \(Z_{n_k}\) has distribution function \(F\) for every \(k\). Part (ii) therefore implies that the preceding bounded-Lipschitz distance converges to zero, a contradiction. \(\square\)

Part (iii) of Proposition 3 implies asymptotic equivalence reduces to uniform weak convergence when the target distribution is fixed.

The next proposition shows that asymptotic equivalence is preserved under uniformly negligible perturbations:

Proposition 4 If \(X_n\stackrel{\mathcal{P}}{\approx}Z_n\) and \(Y_n-X_n=o_{\mathcal{P}}(1)\), then \[ Y_n\stackrel{\mathcal{P}}{\approx}Z_n. \]

Proof. By the triangle inequality, we have \[ \begin{aligned} &\sup_{\theta\in\Theta} \sup_{g\in\mathcal G} \left| \mathbb{E}_\theta[g(Y_n)]-\mathbb{E}_\theta[g(Z_n)] \right| \\ &\qquad\leq \sup_{\theta\in\Theta} \sup_{g\in\mathcal G} \left| \mathbb{E}_\theta[g(Y_n)]-\mathbb{E}_\theta[g(X_n)] \right| \\ &\qquad\quad+ \sup_{\theta\in\Theta} \sup_{g\in\mathcal G} \left| \mathbb{E}_\theta[g(X_n)]-\mathbb{E}_\theta[g(Z_n)] \right|. \end{aligned} \] The second term tends to zero because \(X_n\stackrel{\mathcal{P}}{\approx}Z_n\). To see that the first term also tends to zero, pick \(g\in\mathcal G\) and \(\delta>0\). Then \[ \begin{aligned} \left| \mathbb{E}_\theta[g(Y_n)]-\mathbb{E}_\theta[g(X_n)] \right| &\leq \mathbb{E}_\theta\left|g(Y_n)-g(X_n)\right| \\ &\leq \mathbb{E}_\theta\min\{2,\|Y_n-X_n\|\} \\ &\leq \delta + 2\mathbb{P}_\theta(\|Y_n-X_n\|>\delta). \end{aligned} \] Taking the supremum over \(g\in\mathcal G\) and \(\theta\in\Theta\), then the limit superior as \(n\to\infty\), gives an upper bound of \(\delta\) because \(Y_n-X_n=o_{\mathcal{P}}(1)\). Letting \(\delta\downarrow0\) shows \[ \lim_{n\to\infty} \sup_{\theta\in\Theta} \sup_{g\in\mathcal G} \left| \mathbb{E}_\theta[g(Y_n)]-\mathbb{E}_\theta[g(X_n)] \right| =0. \qquad\square \]

The next proposition records continuous mapping results:

Proposition 5 (i) Suppose \(X_n\stackrel{\mathcal{P}}{\to}X\) and there is a compact set \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^k\) such that \(\mathbb{P}_\theta(X\in K)=1\) for every \(\theta\in\Theta\). If \(h:\mathbb R^k\to\mathbb R^m\) is continuous on an open set containing \(K\), then

\[ h(X_n)\stackrel{\mathcal{P}}{\to}h(X). \]

(ii) Suppose \(X_n\stackrel{\mathcal P}{\approx}Z_n\) and \(Z_n=O_{\mathcal P}(1)\). If \(h:\mathbb R^k\to\mathbb R^m\) is continuous, then

\[ h(X_n) \stackrel{\mathcal P}{\approx} h(Z_n). \]

Proof.

(i) Fix \(\epsilon>0\). By compactness of \(K\) and continuity of \(h\) on a neighborhood of \(K\), there exists \(\delta>0\) such that

\[ z\in\mathbb R^k,\ x\in K,\ \|z-x\|<\delta \quad\Rightarrow\quad \|h(z)-h(x)\|<\epsilon. \]

Since \(X\in K\) almost surely under every \(\mathbb{P}_\theta\),

\[ \left\{ \|h(X_n)-h(X)\|>\epsilon \right\} \cap \{X\in K\} \subseteq \left\{ \|X_n-X\|>\delta/2 \right\}. \]

The right-hand side tends to zero because \(X_n\stackrel{\mathcal{P}}{\to}X\). Since \(\epsilon>0\) was arbitrary, \(h(X_n)\stackrel{\mathcal{P}}{\to}h(X)\).

(ii) Suppose the conclusion fails. Then there are \(\epsilon>0\) and sequences \(n_\ell\to\infty\) and \(\theta_\ell\in\Theta\) such that

\[ \sup_{g\in\mathcal G_m} \left| \mathbb E_{\theta_\ell}[g(h(X_{n_\ell}))] - \mathbb E_{\theta_\ell}[g(h(Z_{n_\ell}))] \right| \geq\epsilon \]

for every \(\ell\). Let \(\mu_\ell\) denote the law of \(Z_{n_\ell}\) under \(\mathbb P_{\theta_\ell}\). We first show that \(\{\mu_\ell\}\) is tight. To do so, fix \(\delta>0\). Because \(Z_n=O_{\mathcal P}(1)\), there exist finite constants \(M\) and \(N\) such that \[ \sup_{\theta\in\Theta} \mathbb P_\theta\left(\|Z_n\|>M\right) <\delta \tag{1}\] for every \(n\geq N\). Since \(n_\ell\to\infty\), there exists an index \(L\) such that \(n_\ell\geq N\) for every \(\ell\geq L\). (1) then gives \[ \mu_\ell\left(\{z\in\mathbb R^k:\|z\|\leq M\}\right) >1-\delta \] for every \(\ell\geq L\). For each \(\ell<L\), we can also construct a compact ball \(K_\ell\) with \(\mu_\ell(K_\ell)>1-\delta\). The finite union \[ K = \{z\in\mathbb R^k:\|z\|\leq M\} \cup \bigcup_{\ell=1}^{L-1}K_\ell \] is compact and satisfies \(\mu_\ell(K)>1-\delta\) for every \(\ell\). Thus the sequence \(\{\mu_\ell\}\) is tight.

By Prokhorov’s theorem, there is a subsequence \(\{\mu_{\ell_j}\}\) that converges weakly to the law of some random vector \(W\) on \(\mathbb R^k\). Part (i) of Proposition 3 and \(X_n\stackrel{\mathcal P}{\approx}Z_n\) imply that \(X_{n_{\ell_j}}\) also converges weakly to \(W\). Because \(h\) is continuous, the continuous mapping theorem implies that both \(h(X_{n_{\ell_j}})\) and \(h(Z_{n_{\ell_j}})\) converge weakly to \(h(W)\). Part (ii) of Proposition 3 then gives

\[ \lim_{j\to\infty} \sup_{g\in\mathcal G_m} \left| \mathbb E_{\theta_{\ell_j}}[g(h(X_{n_{\ell_j}}))] - \mathbb E_{\theta_{\ell_j}}[g(h(Z_{n_{\ell_j}}))] \right| =0, \]

contradicting the preceding lower bound. Thus \(h(X_n)\stackrel{\mathcal P}{\approx}h(Z_n)\). \(\square\)

The final result records a useful joint-convergence rule:

Proposition 6 If \(X_n\stackrel{\mathcal{P}}{\to}X\) and \(Y_n\stackrel{\mathcal{P}}{\to}Y\), then

\[ (X_n,Y_n)\stackrel{\mathcal{P}}{\to}(X,Y). \]

Proof. Suppose \(X_n\stackrel{\mathcal{P}}{\to}X\) and \(Y_n\stackrel{\mathcal{P}}{\to}Y\). For every \(\epsilon>0\),

\[ \left\{ \|(X_n,Y_n)-(X,Y)\|>\epsilon \right\} \subseteq \left\{ \|X_n-X\|>\epsilon/2 \right\} \cup \left\{ \|Y_n-Y\|>\epsilon/2 \right\}. \]

Taking probabilities, taking the supremum over \(\theta\in\Theta\), and using the two marginal convergences proves

\[ (X_n,Y_n)\stackrel{\mathcal{P}}{\to}(X,Y). \qquad\square \]
